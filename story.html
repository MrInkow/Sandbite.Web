Where it came from

Two years after leaving.

Sandbite started two years after leaving Hawaii and moving to Portugal, looking for some of the beauty I had left behind. I was bored of work, bored of doing nothing, and I knew I wanted to learn something, build something that meant anything, and give something back.

The product memory is older than that. Chocolate-covered coffee beans, given to me by Grandma Sara. The thought was simple: we do not really eat coffee over here, and I thought I could make it taste amazing.

After a few attempts, a second memory turned up in the recipe — the date cake from KTA in Waimea. That is where the crumble came from.

Why food

I wanted to watch someone eat it.

I was working on software and felt disconnected from the point of the job. I never saw anybody buy something and get anything back from it straight away.

Food fixed that. You cook it, you hand it to someone, you watch them eat it, and you find out immediately whether they want another one. There is nowhere to hide in that.

02
A line we hold

We don't mine places for marketing.

Hawaii is where this started and that is worth saying once, here, honestly. It is not a costume for the product to wear.

What that means

A flavour doesn't need a story

Places can inspire what we make. They do not get turned into a tourism pitch on the back of a pack because the template has a story slot. If it starts to feel decorative, we stop.

And if it's someone's

We ask first

If a release ever leans on a real person's or a community's story, we talk to them directly and get a yes before it goes anywhere near the brand. If the answer is no, that is the end of it.

Where it is now

Ericeira, by hand.

Every batch is made here. The people who have eaten the most of it are the ones who work outside all day — the bombeiros down the road were among the first to try it and say something honest about it.

That is roughly who it is for. Not athletes in a catalogue. People on a break.

The long game

Giving something back.

The intention is to support the places and causes we end up choosing, and I would want to do it whether or not it was ever mentioned publicly.

Not a claim yet

There is no percentage on this page on purpose. Until we have decided what it is a percentage of, who receives it, how often it is paid and how it gets reported, publishing a number would be marketing rather than a commitment.
